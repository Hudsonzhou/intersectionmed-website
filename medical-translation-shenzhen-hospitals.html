Hospital documents: translation and handover checklist

Keep original records, translated wording and clinical explanations distinct after a visit.

Create a document inventory

DocumentConfirm
Reports and imagesWhich are ready, where to obtain them and whether any remain pending.
Provider instructionsHow to receive an understandable explanation and who answers clinical questions.
Receipts and supporting documentsThe payee, visit details and any insurer-specific document requirements.
TranslationsThe target language, scope, deadline and whether certification is required.

Keep the original with the translation

A translation conveys document wording and should remain linked to the original. It does not independently explain the medical significance of findings. Ask a licensed clinician for interpretation and recommendations.

Check certification requirements first

Hospitals, insurers and other receiving organizations may have different document rules. Ask the receiving organization whether it requires a certified translation, named translator or particular format before arranging the work.

Share only through an agreed channel

Confirm which records are needed, who will receive them and how they should be transferred. Avoid putting detailed records, identity document numbers or payment information in an initial website inquiry.
